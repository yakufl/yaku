“Catastrophe or New Society? The forgotten Latin American contribution to Sustainability Science”
*Degrowth Journal (Under review)*

======

This article recovers the works and ideas of a group of Latin American scientists linked to the Bariloche Foundation between the 1960s and the 1970s. Their work represents a very early predecessor of the current research in sustainability science. I discuss their contributions in three areas: a) an epistemic approach to conducting science that critically discusses development from the Latin American perspective, b) a conception of development centered on human needs which was reflected in the Latin American World Model (LAWM), and c) an innovative view on planning that integrated the problem of political viability. I highlight the similarities with recent works in sustainability, post-growth and social-ecological transformation scholarship. I contend that the current ecological economics research can be nourished and inspired by these Latin American scholars. Finally, I argue that recognizing their contributions is not only an act of justice, but it can also serve to do better science by identifying potential blind spots. In line with the objective of addressing global asymmetries in knowledge production in the context of climate justice, these contributions can help building a postcolonial science.
